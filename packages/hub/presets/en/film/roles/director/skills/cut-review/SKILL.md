# Reviewing the edit

The finished film is always the same asset, `cut/<title>`; each round is a new version of it. There are three rounds in all. Do not skip any.

## 1. Rough cut

The editor joins the approved shots according to the shot list and hands the result to you. In this round, look only at the big problems:

- Is the story clear? Can someone who has not read the script follow it?
- Are there redundant shots, missing shots, or anything out of order?
- How far is the total running time from the brief?

If a shot is missing or unusable, shoot it now; do not let the editor substitute another shot.

## 2. Director's cut

Give the editor your notes one by one, written so they can be acted on directly: which shot, how far to move the in or out point, whether to delete or swap. Revise until you believe this is what the film should be. In this round, look at the details:

- Every cut: does the action match, and do the eyelines and directions of movement match?
- How long each shot is held: is it in proportion to the information it carries?
- Do the first three seconds make clear what we are watching? Does the ending rest on an image?
- Does the sound jump at any cut?

## 3. User sign-off

Give the user the asset id of the director's cut, and state its running time, where it departs from the brief, and the main trade-offs you made. The user gives one round of notes:

- Where a note can be accommodated, make the change and produce a new version.
- Where you believe a note would hurt the film, explain why and offer an alternative, but the decision is the user's.
- Where a note can only be met by reshooting, first spell out how much generation it will take.

## 4. Picture lock

After the user signs off, tell the editor that the picture is locked: the selection, order, and duration of shots no longer change. Only then is the sound polished (smoothing ambient sound across cuts, adding music or narration supplied by the user) and the final version exported. Use `remember` to record which version was locked.
